## Sponsorship check (include only if you need a visa)

Checking before applying is far cheaper than finding out after a tailored resume, cover letter, a long Workday form and interview prep.

**How to check an employer** (e.g. myvisajobs.com / h1bdata.info):
- **(a) The legal entity AND the job family, not the brand.** Large groups sponsor through a few subsidiaries. On the employer page, look at *which job titles* were sponsored. If the list is all engineers / data / architects and you're applying for finance, product or ops, a big total means nothing — skip.
- **(b) The entity on the req must be one that sponsors.** Real case: a req posted under a group's corporate entity that had filed one LCA in nine years, while the group's volume sat in its technology subsidiaries.
- **(c) Salary cross-check.** If the entity's average sponsored salary is well above this req's band, sponsorship likely goes to more senior/technical roles.
- **(d) Same-name trap.** Two different companies can share a name (e.g. a US startup and a foreign fintech). Match the address/HQ before trusting the numbers.
- **(e) Ignore job-board "all work authorizations accepted" tags** (school career portals etc.) — that's the board's category, not the employer's policy.

**"The ATS didn't reject me" ≠ "they sponsor."** An application passing a sponsorship question, or a recruiter reaching out, only means that ATS has no knockout rule. Real case: application passed, recruiter called, interviews happened — and the company said no sponsorship at the end. The entity/title/salary research had predicted it from the start.

**Risk tier → when to ask the recruiter**

| Risk | Signal | Ask |
|---|---|---|
| High | Entity has ~0–9 recent LCAs, OR your job family isn't on its sponsored list, OR band is below its sponsored average, OR the form bundles H-1B with F-1 CPT / STEM OPT paperwork | At the first recruiter contact, before interview prep |
| Medium | Volume exists but your job family is thin | Before the first call ends |
| Low | Perennial sponsor with your job family on the list | At offer stage |

Wording for high risk (polite, but get a clear answer):
> "Before we both invest more time — I'm authorized to work now, on {{STATUS}} through {{END_DATE}}{{EXTENSION_NOTE}}. Longer term I would need sponsorship. Can you tell me whether this role is able to support that?"

Record each company's read in `Target_Companies.xlsx` → "Sponsorship read" so it's done once.
